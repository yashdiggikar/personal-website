Research & Projects

Featured — Ongoing

Prithvi-Dust

Adapting the NASA–IBM Prithvi WxC foundation model for global dust forecasting. Retains the pretrained backbone while adding dust-specific input/output mappings for five size-resolved tracers — surface and column dust mass, aerosol optical properties, emissions, sedimentation, and wet/dry deposition — trained on post-2000 MERRA-2 residuals. Uses attention- and embedding-based attribution to rank meteorological and process controls (winds, PBL height, precipitation, particle size, deposition) on regional and vertical dust bias, and benchmarks against Aurora on events including the 13 June 2022 Iraq sandstorm.

MS Thesis — AMS 2026

AI-Based OSSE Framework

Designed and implemented an AI-based Observing System Simulation Experiment (OSSE) framework spanning four large-scale foundation models — GraphCast (GNN, DeepMind/ECMWF), FourCastNet v2 (SFNO, NVIDIA), Aurora (Transformer, Microsoft), and Prithvi WxC (NASA/IBM) — using ERA5, MERRA-2, and CAMS reanalysis data. Extended autoregressive inference to 60-day global forecasts and injected synthetic and reanalysis-based observations after model spin-up to quantify observing-system value for temperature sounders, wind profilers/lidars, and mass-field constraints. Built a model-accountability and physical-response analysis pipeline — RMSE, NRMSE, ACC, mean bias, cross-variable coupling, synergy metrics, spatial bias-reduction maps, latitude/land-ocean/cloud-regime stratification, and vertical propagation diagnostics — to evaluate how AI weather models assimilate and propagate observational constraints, including Western U.S. high-wind and fire-weather case studies.
